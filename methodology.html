How this was made

This website and its data were created and collected with ChatGPT. We made our best attempt to use reliable sources, especially official election, campaign and organization pages. There may still be inaccuracies or gaps in the data. Check the linked sources before relying on an entry.

What’s covered

Latest expansion checked October 21, 2026, for the November 3 election. Earlier source checks retain their own dates. This release includes 400 candidate and measure listings across 173 contests, with 1,200 attributed claims from 47 entities. Federal, governor and all 75 legislative contest rosters are included. Local and endorsement coverage remain partial and are not equally extensive across political viewpoints.

November election-office rosters and voters’ pamphlets identify candidates and contests. Local coverage focuses on selected races across multiple counties and 12 measures. Some local records rely on filing lists that still need a final certified-ballot check. County filters show known district overlap, not your personal ballot.

The OLCV coverage audit includes 105 of the 106 positions on its official general-election slate. One is withheld: OLCV lists Mai Vang for Salem Ward 6, while the certified November candidate list names Betsy Vega. That conflict needs clarification. See the entity-by-entity source review for expanded slates and remaining gaps.

The latest source review compares full issuer lists with all 22 state November pamphlets and campaign endorsement pages. Older claims and chapter-level decisions stay distinct. When a candidate has multiple sources, the info card preserves them with their own dates and labels.

Published voter guides include conservative, labor, environmental, business and other viewpoints. Endorsements and recommendations use the same simple support filter; their exact labels stay in the cards. Questionnaire participation, tax pledges and ratings are not automatically treated as support. The guide does not rank candidates by endorsement totals.

How sources are read

Source attribution labels and their meanings
LabelMeaning
Issuer-confirmedPublished by the endorsing entity itself.
Campaign-reportedListed by a campaign, including in a campaign-submitted voters’ pamphlet statement. The election office does not verify that claim.
General / Primary / Stage unspecifiedThe election stage supported by the source. A primary claim does not automatically become a general-election endorsement.

These labels describe the evidence, not a candidate’s quality or a numerical confidence score. Every claim links to its source and check date. Recommendations, including OEA’s recommendations to NEA, are explained in the info cards. Party nominations are shown separately and are not counted as endorsements.

Missing support means no qualifying claim was verified here; it does not mean opposition. Contributions, photographs, event appearances, party affiliation and scorecard grades alone do not establish an endorsement. Personal endorsements do not imply support from the person’s government office.

Matching candidates and maintaining the guide

Names are matched using the race, office and candidate context. Obvious spelling variations, shortened names and accents are treated as the same person when that context is clear. Material conflicts about the person or office require further checking.

Candidate info cards link to an official campaign or candidate page where one could be verified: 203 of 388 candidates currently have a link. The remaining cards say that a campaign page was not found. Ballotpedia background profiles are linked for 367 candidates where a matching profile was found. Ballotpedia is not used as primary endorsement evidence. The downloadable data retains the source used to identify each campaign page.

This is a research snapshot, not an automatically updated service. Corrections should update both data files and the change log. The earlier ChatGPT conversation supplied research leads, not evidence for endorsements.

Have you found gaps or errors in the data? Know an endorsing entity that should be added? Please create an issue on Github.

Where to check

Start with the source links in each info card. Roster references include the Oregon Secretary of State’s November pamphlets, official offices and district overlap, and Multnomah County filings. Endorsement evidence comes from organizations’ own pages, explicit campaign claims and publications’ own editorial recommendations.

More research is needed across conservative, independent, business, labor, environmental and other groups, as well as prominent individuals and local races. No inference should be drawn about an entity or candidate that is missing.

Privacy and accessibility

No accounts, advertising, analytics, external fonts or tracking scripts. Filters are stored in the shareable URL. The guide supports keyboard navigation, skip links, labeled switches, visible focus, result announcements and info cards that close with Escape. Text follows your browser’s font size. Colors follow supported system preferences for light or dark appearance and higher or lower contrast, including forced colors; no extra settings panel is needed. Reduced-motion preferences are respected. Read the accessibility review and testing limits. No address is collected. File links, filenames and document titles are remembered only during this visit to warn before a repeat request. Closing this browser tab clears that memory; reloading the page does not. This uses no cookies, permanent identifiers or server-side tracking. The site cannot verify whether a download completed. File links show the source filename when supplied; your browser may choose a different saved name. Known page numbers refer to the PDF page count.
