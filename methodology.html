EVIDENCE BEFORE INFERENCE

How this guide is made.

We document public support and explicit opposition without recommending candidates, ranking their quality, or treating endorsement totals as a score. Research checked October 19, 2026; election November 3, 2026.

Coverage is partial

This research release includes 153 candidate/measure listings across 73 contests and 284 attributed claims from 40 entities. It includes the U.S. Senate, all six U.S. House districts and governor, with selective legislative and local coverage. It is not a complete Oregon candidate, measure or endorsement inventory. There is no claim of equal coverage by political viewpoint. Search results reflect research availability, not a candidate’s eligibility or popularity.

Federal and governor choices were matched to November election-office lists. Legislative records were matched to the November pamphlets for Lane, Marion, Multnomah, Washington and the eastern county booklet. Local records use the corresponding official pamphlet or Multnomah’s November filing page. A filing list is weaker than a final certified roster; the Multnomah page says last updated August 21. Local coverage therefore requires a final certified-roster recheck before this guide is promoted as a complete local ballot reference.

Legislative county filters reflect known overlap from the pamphlet jurisdictions, with additional overlap from the official open-offices document. They are a discovery aid, not an address-to-ballot lookup. Local coverage currently focuses on selected Lane, Union and Multnomah races and five local measures. Many districts, counties, judicial contests and city races remain outside this dataset.

Evidence labels

LabelWhat it means
Issuer-confirmedThe entity publishes its own support or opposition. This is source attribution, not a numerical confidence estimate.
Campaign-reportedA campaign lists the entity or individual on its own page or in its submitted voters’ pamphlet statement. Independent issuer confirmation has not been established here.
GeneralThe source explicitly concerns the November/general election, or appears in a November campaign statement.
2026 · stage unspecifiedA current 2026 list does not consistently identify primary versus general stage. These claims remain separately filterable; they are not silently relabeled general-election endorsements.
No endorsement verifiedNo qualifying claim is included in this dataset. It does not mean opposition, refusal, neutrality or lack of support elsewhere.

Explicit “No” measure positions and “Do not rank” candidate recommendations are displayed only when stated by the issuer. Party ballot nominations appear separately under each candidate’s name and are never counted as endorsement claims. Primary-cycle claims retain a visible primary label and their original context in the info card. Candidate support shares one simple view; recommendations are explained inside the info cards rather than adding new controls. OEA’s federal recommendations to NEA are explicitly labeled as recommendations, not endorsements. A contribution, attendance, photograph, scorecard grade or party affiliation alone is not treated as an endorsement.

Source selection and normalization

  1. Identify the race and November roster in a state/county election source. Use ballot names and listed party nominations, including cross-nominations. Include candidates without verified endorsements in covered races.
  2. Read the endorsing entity’s current page, or a campaign’s explicit claim. Retain the source URL, attribution category, election stage and check date per claim.
  3. Match names by race as well as name; recognize abbreviated names and diacritics without conflating people or committees. Keep differently named organizations separate.
  4. Summarize entity purpose and office context briefly. Campaign-hosted evidence remains campaign-reported even when printed in a government-hosted pamphlet. Election offices do not fact-check campaign endorsement claims.
  5. Publish corrections to data.js and data.json together. Record changed/withdrawn claims with their original evidence in a change log before removal; this release has no known withdrawals and no automated refreshing.

Discrepancies and unresolved work

  • The Umatilla Republican slate lists Cliff Bentz under House District 1; the election-office roster places him in District 2. That slate’s Bentz claim is withheld pending clarification rather than repeating the wrong district. Other independently sourced AG-PAC support for Bentz is included.
  • The nurses’ list says “Bri William,” while the local election filing names Bri Williams. That particular ONA claim is withheld pending clarification. Portland for All’s independently published Bri Williams recommendation is included.
  • The ONA page spells House District 52’s name “Hank Sandars.” That claim is withheld; AFSCME and the election roster consistently identify Hank Sanders.
  • AG-PAC uses variant spellings for Amanda Staehely and some other candidates. Ambiguous mappings are withheld. Lesly M Munoz / Lesly Muñoz, Myrna A Munoz / Myrna Muñoz and Daniel Loc Nguyen / Daniel Nguyen are matched by office.
  • The Lane roster prints “Jill Cuardros,” while its candidate index spells “Jill Cuadros.” This release retains the roster spelling and flags the discrepancy in candidate details.
  • The earlier chat’s tables were truncated and did not provide independently usable evidence for all claims. They are a research lead, not a data source.
  • Further source work is needed for Oregon Right to Life PAC, Oregon Family Council PAC, Taxpayers Association of Oregon, Forests for Oregon, housing/building PACs, Basic Rights Oregon PAC, Color PAC, Building Power for Communities of Color, East County Rising, Humane Voters Oregon, Next Up Action Fund, Safety & Justice Oregon, Stand for Children and Willamette Women Democrats. No conclusion about their current endorsements is implied. Other earlier entities appear here only for specifically sourced claims, not their entire slates.
  • Prominent individual coverage is currently limited to explicit campaign-listed claims. A public official’s personal endorsement does not imply endorsement by their government office.

Privacy and accessibility

No advertising, analytics, accounts, external fonts or tracking scripts. Filters are stored in the URL and can be shared. Standard keyboard controls, labeled switches, visible focus indicators, an Escape-dismissable information dialog and screen-reader result counts are provided. No address is collected. For exact ballot eligibility and complete choices, use your election office.
